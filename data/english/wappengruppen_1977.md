# Wappengruppen [1977]

## **Wappengruppen des Adels im Odenwald-Spessart-Raum**

Wolfert, A. F.:  
(Beiträge z. Gesch. des Odenwaldes u. s. Randlandschaften II.)


**N 1. von Uissigheim**

The namesake village is located about 8 km northeast of Schweinberg, and just 3 km further in the same direction, the Gamburg lies above the Tauber.

The earliest mention is found in 1178 among the witnesses of the Bishop of Würzburg and the Counts of Wertheim regarding the parish and tithe of Reicholzheim, including the brothers Arnold and Konrad, sons of "Rupertus de Ussenkeim," as well as Herbord of Uissigheim. The first names Arnold and Konrad, and especially Eberhard, continue to appear in the family from 1212 onward. In 1323, Crafto of Uissigheim was the Wertheim bailiff in Schweinberg. The family seems to have become extinct by the mid-16th century.

The shield of their coat of arms is divided into five sections vertically and one horizontally in silver and red; the crest shows two swan necks, the right one in silver and the left one in red. In older seals from 1286, 1329, and 1335, however, only two swan busts are depicted, with the 1329 seal showing the helmet with the crest in the seal field. (Illustration 39)


**N 2. von Rosenberg**

H. Bauer and W. Möller both view the von Rosenberg family as a branch of the von Uissigheim family, which split off shortly before 1300 and settled in the village of Rosenberg, located 12 km west of Boxberg, and subsequently named themselves after it. The first names Konrad, Eberhard, and Arnold continue to appear among them. They still held Wertheim fiefs in the 15th century.

The family achieved such prosperity that in 1381, four brothers and cousins jointly purchased the Lordship of Boxberg, and each entrusted their quarter of it as a fief to the Palatinate. The family became extinct in 1632. Their coat of arms is continued in the multi-fielded coat of arms of the Counts and Princes of Löwenstein-Wertheim.

Their coat of arms resembles that of the von Uissigheim family, but the order of the colors in the shield is reversed; in the crest, a red rose is placed between the swan necks. However, in the oldest seals, the two swan busts appear, with those from 1323 and 1388 even explicitly placed within a shield, such as in the seal of Eberhard von Rosenberg, bailiff of (Tauber-)Bischofsheim, in 1323. Additionally, a helmet seal from 1348 shows the two swan necks, while another from 1361 also includes the rose.


**N 3. von Schweigern (Schweigerer)**

The earliest mentions in the Codex of the Hirsau Monastery, with the brothers Swigger and Bertilo de Sweigern (1105-20), do not belong here but rather to a dynastic family from Swabia.

The Franconian family named themselves after the eastern neighboring village of Boxberg. Shortly after 1200, they appear in documents, starting in 1220 with Konrad von Schweigern as a vassal of the Lords of Bocksberg and in 1243 as the Würzburg Hofschultheiß (chief magistrate). Even earlier, since 1203, Gotfried von Schweigern is recorded as a canon of Würzburg Cathedral, and from 1239 as dean of the cathedral chapter, † 1248.

The ties to the Bishopric of Würzburg remained strong; in the 15th century, a branch of the family resided in Schwanfeld (20 km northeast of Würzburg). However, they should not be confused with the original local nobility "von Schwanfeld" there. Initially, members of this family served the Counts of Henneberg, later entering the service of the bishop. No distinct coat of arms is known for them, but their relatives, who were named after the neighboring village "von Wipfeld," had a red shield with a silver swan, clearly alluding to Schwanfeld.

The von Schweigern family in Schwanfeld became extinct in 1544, and those in Mühlbach am Main (opposite Karlstadt) in 1575.

The coat of arms seems to have originally been a helmet crest, which was later placed on the shield: A silver swan's torso with a red dorsal comb, set against a blue shield, adorned with rooster or peacock feathers—this is typical for helmet crests around 1300, often used with lion torsos. The same image is repeated on the helmet.
