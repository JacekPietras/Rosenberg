## **TABVLA CCCCVI. A.**

From the extinct Lords of Rosenberg in the second main line.

* **Georg** von Rosenberg zu Schipff and Boxberg, continued this branch line (Tab. CCCCV.). The High Princely Brandenburg bailiff of Schwabach, an enemy of Bishop Rudolph of Würzburg, as well as of the city of Halle in Swabia, came to the tournament in Heidelberg in 1481, to the one in Onoldsbach in 1484, and to the one in Bamberg in 1486, where he was on the side of the Franconian knighthood of Grießwertel. Bishop Rudolph of Würzburg, at his persevering request, confessed to him 400 florins before Lord Marquard von Thurn and his heirs. Rhenish on his portion of the tithe at Rosenberg and on all his goods, rents, interest, annuities, dues, and other rights, along with the authorities in village and field, on Monday Anthony in the year 1480. In his time, Boxberg Castle was besieged and captured for 41 days by Archbishop Adolph of Mainz, Bishop Rudolf of Würzburg, and Count Palatine Frederick, with a large army of many local servants, quos tum cives regni nominare solebant Latini. It then belonged to him and his brothers Arnold and Michael. Seventy noblemen were garrisoned there, but all escaped untouched. After the conquest, the castle was returned to the Lords of Rosenberg, but under very strict conditions. At the same time, the Imperial Knights' Castle of Schipff, which had previously been occupied by the Imperial Nobles of Dottenheim, but then by the Lords of Rosenberg, was captured and razed after an eight-day siege.
