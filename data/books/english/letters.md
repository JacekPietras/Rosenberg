L. The undersigned 137 Counts, Lords, Knights, and Squires unite to form a Tournament Society under the following conditions. Schweinfurt, September 23, 1387. We, the undersigned counts, lords, knights, and squires, openly acknowledge by this open letter for us and our heirs, that we, for good reasons and for the sake of friendship, 10. have amicably united and agreed upon a society with such a distinction as is written hereinafter. Firstly, that each of us shall faithfully assist the other in the tournament against anyone who wishes us harm, without deceit. Also, we shall dress in one and the same attire, as often and as much as we decide, for the sake of the society, as the king and the two decide, which are then set for that year. We shall also participate in a tournament as often and as much as we decide in the chapter, as is written hereinafter. If any of us, our counts, lords, knights, should be given a thank-offering, be it for a tournament or a joust, he may let it be described and placed in a suitable location. For this, we shall all help with the chest, with the sword and with the spear, if the court so requires, as the arbitrator decides and as is written hereinafter. We shall also come to the chapter twice a year and seek a chapter in Würzburg. If it happens that our companion could not or was not able to get safe-conduct in the same city, then the king and the two, who are appointed for this, may lay it in a suitable place where one can get safe-conduct, and whoever cannot come under us, be it for bodily reasons or if he is sick or if lords hinder him or for other reasons that rightfully hinder and impede him, he shall send his power to the chapter, and if he is a count or a lord, he shall send ten gulden, a knight shall send three gulden and a squire also three gulden to the cost of the chapter. Also, a count or a lord in our society shall give as much as the king determines for our use, be it with women and with servants, And also the two, who belong there, excluding anyone who is from outside the land, who is in this society, needs not give the aforementioned gulden. If anyone hears or learns from another that his honor has been severely touched, he may clearly declare it in the chapter, and the one who is accused or has the matter against him, shall be spoken to and judged without delay, how he shall then conduct himself in the society. But if he does not do this, then he shall abandon the society, when the king and the two, or the majority among them, decide, and he shall never again take any society nor hold it, nor with anyone else. Furthermore, if anyone in our society wishes to leave due to ill will, he shall not again enter any society in any manner or way as stated above. Furthermore, if any of us go to battle or war for the sake of the tournament, we shall all help them in their castle and with their swords, so that the king and the two, with or among them, acknowledge that it concerns the tournament. Also, each of us shall faithfully answer to the other in all places where he hears that no harm or disrespect has come to his companions. Also, each of us in this society shall not, without deceit, concern himself with the tournament. Also, we shall all elect a king to the chapter, and the king shall have full authority over us and the society. Furthermore, if any dispute or disagreement arises among us, it shall remain with those who are written below. They shall then carry out the dispute and disease of their body, from their own, inherited, goods and fiefs, in the customs where it belongs by right. Also, if we take possession of a court, and a thank-offering is given to it, then the same shall bring as many women to the court as the king and the two, who are appointed for this year, decide. If the number of women is not sufficient, they shall bring as many gulden to the court as they decide. We shall all help with the chest, with the sword and with the spear, if the court so requires, as the arbitrator decides and as is written hereinafter. We shall also come to the chapter twice a year and seek a chapter in Würzburg. If it happens that our companion could not or was not able to get safe-conduct in the same city, then the king and the two, who are appointed for this, may lay it in a suitable place where one can get safe-conduct, and whoever cannot come under us, be it for physical reasons or if he is sick or if lords hinder him or for other reasons that rightfully hinder and impede him, he shall send his power to the chapter, and if he is a count or a lord, he shall send ten gulden, a knight shall send three gulden and a squire also three gulden to the cost of the chapter. Because fieldwork is now beginning, they can only send 60 men, which he should be content with. — (Monday, Our Dear Feast Day Annunciation) March 26. — A copy of the feud letter from the Count Palatine to Jörg von Rosenberg, dated Monday after Paul's Conversion, January 29, is available. d) The city declares feud to the allies of Count Palatine Frederick against Junker Jörg von Rosenberg. — (Friday before the feast of St. Paul) March 30. e) Lutz Schott to the city: announces that with God's help, Boxberg was captured last Thursday night and that Schüpf (Schupffe), where they are now moving, is something they hope to soon take. Their citizens do not want to go without a letter from the council, so the council should write to them. — (Maundy Thursday) April 19. f) The city replied that it would gladly fulfill his wish, but would first like to know who the commons and co-heirs of Schüpf were, and whether the Count Palatine had become their enemy on his own behalf or as an aider of another. Initially, it had only committed itself against Jörg von Rosenberg. — (Holy Thursday) April 20. g) Schott replied that Jörg von Rosenberg had declared war on part of Schüpf, and the Count Palatine had also declared war on the other co-heirs, including Michel von Rosenberg, who owned the largest part of Schüpf and to whom the Bishop of Würzburg had already declared war. Likewise, the Archbishop of Mainz was an enemy of Jörg von Dottenheim. The city should send its letter of feud to Boxberg through its servant Hans Seldner and have its own people march before Schüpf. The co-heirs were listed on an enclosed slip of paper. (They are: Walther Zobel, Michel von Rosenberg, Philipp, Hans and Wilhelm von Dottenheim, Arnold, and Friedrich von Rosenberg the Younger.) — Boxberg (Easter evening) April 21. h) Count Palatine Friedrich to the city: requests that they support Count Eberhard of Württemberg, who has now become his helper against the Rosenbergs, as if it concerned them personally. — Germersheim (Easter evening) April 21.


### **1473 April 9, Heidelberg** 

Heilbronn 238, IV7. Or. Aussen Adresse und Siegel unter Papierdecke.

Count Palatine Frederick to Heilbronn: Jörg von Rosenberg recently ambushed his steward, Eberhard von Gemmingen, while riding to Gochsen (Gossen) near Heilbronn, which would have been impossible without shelter. Since the city allows its troops to roam daily, especially now during the Frankfurt Fair, and Rosenberg has already made the area unsafe before, this is surprising to him. He asks him to be more careful so that he and his troops suffer no further harm near Heilbronn.
