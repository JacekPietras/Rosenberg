# Monuments with two swan heads/necks as the shield charge

## Finding

Applying the user's orientation and placement criteria—two swan heads/necks face outward, away from one another, as in BSB Cod.icon. 309, and appear as a shield/device charge rather than merely as a crest—I have not verified a qualifying Franconian monument in the sources checked. The outward-facing pair does occur in Rosenberg/Uissigheim monument heraldry, but as the helmet crest above a striped shield. That is a meaningful visual parallel to the Cod.icon. 309 motif, while remaining a different heraldic placement.

## Rosenberg / Uissigheim memorials checked

| Monument | Evidence and date | Result under strict criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Arnold of Uissigheim, parish church at Uissigheim | The grave slab is dated by its inscription to 1336. A local historical account reproduces the 1910 description: the striped shield is shown separately, while the helmet with two swan necks is placed sideways as a pillow because of lack of space. The source describes the swan-head directions as away from the helmet, matching the outward-facing orientation of Cod.icon. 309. The same account describes the matching arms embedded in the south aisle wall at Bronnbach Abbey and a broken grave slab with the same arms in the east cloister. [Uissigheim historical page, quoting Werr (1910)](https://uissigheim.de/geschichte/ritter-arnold/) | **Not a shield-charge match, but a confirmed outward-facing crest parallel.** The striped shield is the armorial charge; the outward-facing pair belongs to the helmet crest. The Uissigheim grave slab and Bronnbach examples attest the same Rosenberg/Uissigheim crest tradition. |
| Rosenberg funerary monuments at Wölchingen | The local/Rosenberg monument corpus refers to these as Rosenberg arms; no image or description located in this pass showed two swan heads/necks as a shield charge. The family arms have the divided, repeatedly parted red/silver shield and paired swan necks as crest. | **Exclude unless a particular carving proves otherwise.** Consistent with the user's correction: the known Wölchingen monuments are the ordinary Rosenberg arms, not the two-swan shield motif. |
| Konrad von Rosenberg and Adelheid von Hirschhorn, an inscribed stone reported in 1892 | The 1892 *Zeitschrift für die Geschichte des Oberrheins* describes a stone 1.36 m wide and 2.04 m high, then in the cellar of the house/business of master mason Leonhard Herion. The reported death year is damaged/uncertain in the transcription, so do not assign a precise date from it. |

The official town-history page independently summarizes the Uissigheim arms as a divided/parted shield with a helmet and two swan necks, and names Rosenberg, Höchheim, Thalheim, Rannenberg/Kannenberg, Randersacker, Reinstein, and Rieneck as families said to share the pattern. That is a lead list for family-armorial research, not evidence of monuments with swan charges. [Uissigheim history](https://uissigheim.de/geschichte/)

## Other monument leads examined

- **Anna Maria von Vintler, Mals/Malles, 1809:** a digitized antiquarian description says her crowned quartered shield has two swan heads facing and beaking one another in fields 1 and 4, with horns in fields 2 and 3. This does **not** match the required outward-facing orientation and is outside Franconia. It is excluded, not a match. The source gives her monument at the church in **Mals/Malles**, South Tyrol, and death date 8 June 1809. [Ferdinandeum publication, pp. 36–37](https://www.zobodat.at/pdf/VeroeffFerd_3_20_0001-0042.pdf). The scan/descriptive text, rather than a current site photograph, was checked here.
- **Georg Kühlewein (1593–1656), St. Johannis, Magdeburg:** a secondary historical compilation reports his grave inscription and arms in the church. The two swan heads belong to the helmet crest and hold a grape between them. The shield charge is a vessel/cooling tub with handled wine jugs, so this does not qualify. The family is reported as having a Franconian/Würzburg branch, which makes it a potentially relevant comparative name only. [Zieringer compilation, PDF](https://z-m-a.de/Dokumente_ZMA/Zieringer-Druckhefte/Druckheft3.pdf); heraldic summary: [Kühlewein](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BChlewein_%28Adelsgeschlecht%29).

## Conclusion and next useful checks

The monument evidence confirms **outward-facing paired swan necks in the Rosenberg/Uissigheim crest tradition**, clearly described on Arnold of Uissigheim's 1336 grave slab and on the matching Bronnbach wall armorial. These are a close visual orientation parallel to Cod.icon. 309, but the monuments place the pair in the crest above the parted/striped shield. The 1892 Rosenberg–Hirschhorn stone also has paired necks as a crest, although that source does not state their orientation. No Franconian monument has yet been verified with the outward-facing pair as the shield/device charge. Vintler is not a match because its heads face inward toward one another. The blue two-neck shield in BSB Cod.icon. 309 remains a separate armorial witness whose bearer should be identified from its folio caption and neighboring entries; its charge should not be projected onto the Rosenberg monuments without evidence.

For follow-up, the strongest leads are (1) locate and inspect the current physical/digital record for the 1892 stone, while retaining its explicit crest classification; (2) search monuments for the named regional families using images, not surname-only catalogue hits; and (3) identify the Cod.icon. 309 bearer, then search that family and its spouse/ancestor lines for funerary monuments.
