# Rosenberg in the Palatine fief book: paired swan heads

## Finding

The 1471 Palatine fief book is a primary source for Jörg von Rosenberg’s fief, but this research pass could **not verify the claimed two-swan-head shield in the manuscript image**. Clemmensen’s page-by-page analysis assigns that device to fol. 128r, describes a complex counterchanged shield with a rose between two swan heads, and identifies Jürgen (Jörg) von Rosenberg. The publicly accessible archive scan that can be tied to Jörg’s entry is visibly fol. 128a and contains text only; the associated image result is fol. 123 and depicts a different shield. The evidence therefore supports Clemmensen’s catalog assertion, but the visual match to fol. 128r remains unresolved.

The reported 1323 arms—Gules, two silver swan heads addorsed—are **not a contemporary blazon printed in the 1471 book**. They are Clemmensen’s retrospective statement, supported in the entry’s bibliography by later genealogical and heraldic literature, especially Walther Möller, *Stamm-Tafeln westdeutscher Adels-Geschlechter im Mittelalter*, vol. II, part 2, pp. 188–191. I have not found a digitized 1323 seal or charter image that independently verifies this precise charge and tincture. Thus 1471 is the secure visual evidence; 1323 remains a plausible but not yet primary-source-verified earlier attestation.

## The manuscript and the image

The original is Generallandesarchiv Karlsruhe, **GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057**, the *Lehnbuch des Pfalzgrafen Friedrich I. (mit Miniaturen und farbigen Wappen aller Vasallen)*. The archive’s catalog link is [GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/plink/?f=4-3650588). LEO-BW identifies the volume as Friedrich I’s 1471 fief book and explains that fief books record the status of granted fiefs; its catalog overview lists the volume at GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057. See [LEO-BW, “Lehensbücher und Lehensregister”](https://www.leo-bw.de/fr/themenmodul/sudwestdeutsche-archivalienkunde/archivaliengattungen/amtsbucher/lehensbucher-und-lehensregister).

Clemmensen’s open-access catalog gives this entry under no. 126:

> “128r A G per fess & paly cch … no crest ornament OX-AG rose betw 2 swan’s heads”

He then identifies “Jürgen von Rosenberg” and says the family “was a cadet of Uissigheim, separated late 13C, noted 1323 with {Gu 2 swan’s heads addorsed Ar}.” This is the clearest readily searchable record for the folio: [Clemmensen, *An armorial of fief holders in the County Palatine*, fol. 128r entry](https://www.armorial.dk/german/PfalzLehen.pdf#page=47), printed PDF p. 47 (item 126; the “173” following the folio reference is the image/plate number in his catalog). His bibliography for the family is on the same page.

The catalog is explicit that the swans are on the shield and that there is no crest ornament. On its face, the entry therefore meets the user’s charge-location criterion. The manuscript itself is a colored armorial record, and the archival unit is described as containing colored arms. I inspected the archive’s public image explorer and high-resolution scans in the Jörg von Rosenberg result group. One scan is clearly marked fol. 123 (archival image 1) and shows a shield unrelated to paired swan heads; the other is clearly marked fol. 128a and is a text page whose heading identifies Jörg von Rosenberg. Neither scan shows the asserted arms. The archive item group’s caption is not itself proof that each image is the matching heraldic illustration. The exact fol. 128r image is therefore not securely identified from the accessible result group. Direct visual confirmation of the charge, orientation, and tinctures is outstanding.

There is a relevant historical change of arms. Clemmensen transcribes the 1471 shield as “per fess & paly counterchanged,” and says the old arms were Gules, two silver swan heads addorsed, while the family—like Uissigheim—changed to the counterchanged pattern. In the 1471 shield the rose is positioned between the swan heads. “Addorsed” means back-to-back, hence outward-facing. This makes the entry materially different from the familiar Rosenberg/Uissigheim image where paired swan necks are a helmet crest over a striped shield.

## The 1323 claim and the source chain

Clemmensen’s source chain has two levels:

1. **Primary visual source for 1471:** GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057, fol. 128r. The archive identifies the fief book as containing miniature illustrations and colored arms; the 1471 date is corroborated by LEO-BW and archival scholarship.
2. **Secondary historical reconstruction for 1323:** Clemmensen’s text states the earlier blazon and cites Möller, *Stamm-Tafeln* II/2, pp. 188–191, among the family references. Clemmensen does not cite a 1323 charter number or describe a specific seal impression in the entry. His concise note may synthesize Möller and other reference works; the exact origin of the date and tinctures remains to be traced.

Alfred F. Wolfert’s specialist work on Odenwald-Spessart heraldic groups is a relevant regional reference for the Uissigheim/Rosenberg relationship: “Wappengruppen des Adels im Odenwald-Spessart-Raum,” in *Beiträge zur Erforschung des Odenwalds und seiner Randlandschaften II* (1977), pp. 325–406, especially p. 378, as cited by the Uissigheim article. The Uissigheim municipal history reproduces an extract from Lauf and Uihlein’s 1966 local history and describes the shared red-and-silver counterchanged shield with the two swan-neck crest. These sources corroborate the regional family relationship and the better-known crest tradition, but do not independently establish the Rosenberg 1323 shield blazon. See [Uissigheim municipal history](https://uissigheim.de/geschichte/) and [Wolfert citation surfaced in the Uissigheim arms reference](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uissigheim_(Adelsgeschlecht)).

Other accessible evidence supports a close relationship between the lines without proving the specific earlier blazon. A local historical study of Arnold von Uissigheim notes a 1332 charter sealed by Wiprecht von Rosenberg and discusses the Rosenberg line as separating from the Uissigheim family while retaining heraldic kinship; it does not identify that seal’s device as the two-head shield. See [Uissigheim, “Arnold von Uissigheim (‘König Armleder’)”](https://uissigheim.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ARNOLD-VON-UISSIGHEIM.pdf). The municipal account likewise says Rosenberg and Uissigheim shared the familiar quartered shield and swan-neck crest, but attributes the statement to a 1966 Heimatbuch, not to a medieval seal reading.

## Interpretation and confidence

- **High confidence:** The original is GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057, a 1471 fief book.
- **High confidence as a report of Clemmensen, not as an independently checked manuscript reading:** his entry 126 assigns two swan heads and an intervening rose to Jörg von Rosenberg at fol. 128r, without crest ornament.
- **Unresolved:** I could not map Clemmensen’s fol. 128r citation to a visible image of the shield. The archive result group captioned Jörg von Rosenberg includes fol. 128a text and a fol. 123 armorial image that is not the claimed swan-head shield. This is a real image/folio discrepancy, not confirmation of the blazon.
- **Medium confidence:** The heads are outward-facing in the reported historical form because Clemmensen says “addorsed”; this orientation has not been visually checked on the 1471 shield.
- **Medium confidence:** A simpler red-field, silver-heads addorsed form was attributed to the family in 1323. Clemmensen reports it, but the available citation chain is secondary and no contemporaneous 1323 image or seal catalog entry was located here.
- **High confidence:** The Rosenberg family was considered a cadet of Uissigheim by Clemmensen and later regional accounts. The precise genealogy and separation date should be checked against the cited medieval genealogy and charter literature if needed.

Two linked questions remain open: what primary evidence underlies “noted 1323,” and which physical scan is Clemmensen’s cited 128r. Therefore describe 1323 as a secondary-source report and the 1471 device as a cataloged—but in this pass not visually verified—armorial depiction. It is a promising Franconian parallel to Cod.icon. 309’s outward-facing shield pair, but should not be presented as a confirmed visual match until the folio is located.

## Image-level verification log (October 2026)

The Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg image explorer for Bestand 10798 (GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057) exposes a result group captioned “Jörg von Rosenberg.” I downloaded and inspected both image results at high resolution. The first has a visible folio number 123 and a colored shield with a divided field and star; it does not contain swan heads. The second is visibly marked “128a” and is a text leaf. Its heading names Jörg von Rosenberg, but no armorial shield is drawn on that leaf. Thus this group verifies the name/entry association but not Clemmensen’s 128r heraldic reading. The catalog group’s label appears to bind images to an entry, but the two scans do not straightforwardly correspond to “fol. 128r with arms.”

Archive entry points: [image explorer, Bestand 10798](https://www2.landesarchiv-bw.de/ofs21/olf/bildexplorer.php?bestand=10798); [catalog permalink](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/plink/?f=4-3650588). Clemmensen gives “128r” and “173” (his image/plate reference) but the accessible archive result group’s foliation does not yield a scan of the swan-head shield. The high-resolution copies inspected in this pass were local working files; the public explorer is the reproducible route. I did not locate an independent digitization or Wolfert’s 1986 plate scan that resolves the mismatch.

This does not disprove Clemmensen’s reading: the relevant page may be uncaptured, hidden in another archive image set, or referenced under a different archival foliation. It does mean the previous claim that 1471 was a “strong primary visual witness” was too confident. The defensible formulation is: **Clemmensen reports the 1471 Jörg von Rosenberg entry as bearing paired swan heads on the shield; the publicly identified scans inspected here do not yet independently demonstrate that device.**
