# Two outward-facing swan heads as a shield charge: review of armorial images in `images.json`

## Result

Searching `data/images/images.json` for the region name `goses` finds exactly two records: BSB Cod.icon. 309, fol. 51r, and BSB Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573. The 309 page is a **confirmed outward-facing pair of swan heads/necks on the shield**. The 307 page is a **strong but species-uncertain parallel**: the shield contains two outward-facing long-necked bird heads, but the monochrome drawing does not let me confidently distinguish swans/geese from a stylized proboscis-like charge. I therefore count 309 as the verified match and 307 as a lead requiring specialist/source-text confirmation.

This is a review of the individual images and folio samples entered in the catalog, not a folio-by-folio search of every manuscript represented by a Drive collection. The catalog has whole-volume/collection links without a folio locator, so those links cannot establish that every page of those books has been searched.

## Scope checked

I visually checked the armorial page images presently listed in `data/images/images.json` outside Cod.icon. 307 and 309, including the catalog samples from Gelre, Bellenville, Donaueschingen, Ingeram, the St. Christoph and Hubertus books, Scheibler, Codex Bergshammar, the Constance chronicles, the Pfälzisches Wappenlehnbuch, Eichstätt, Grünenberg, Wernigerode, Jörg Rugen, Colmar, Cod.icon. 308/311/312, Codex Rossianus, Siebmacher, the Würzburg-Franconian armorial sheets, the Miltenberger Wappenbuch, and other listed tournament and armorial collections. This names the image sets represented in the catalog; it does not claim exhaustive inspection of every folio in their source manuscripts.

The catalog entries provide the source and image links. For exact folio locators, consult the corresponding records in [`data/images/images.json`](../../data/images/images.json). Where that file has no folio field, I have left the page locator explicitly unknown rather than infer one from the image.

## Conclusion

There is one unequivocal shield-charge example in the region-tagged records: Cod.icon. 309, fol. 51r, captioned “Himmelreich.” Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573 is a genuine additional candidate with outward-facing long-necked bird heads on its shield, but the species is uncertain in the monochrome image. The numerous other sampled paired swans in the catalog are crest depictions and must remain separate from these shield charges. Further identification of the Cod.icon. 307 charge depends on a blazon, clearer colored witness, or corroborating armorial.
