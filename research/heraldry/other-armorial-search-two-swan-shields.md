# Search for further armorials with two outward-facing swan heads in the shield

**Question.** Are there armorial depictions beyond the two images already identified in BSB Cod.icon. 309, fol. 51r, and Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573, where two swan heads/necks face outward and appear as charges on the shield itself?

## Result

I have not verified a third armorial page that meets all three conditions: (1) two swan heads/necks, (2) their heads face outward, and (3) the pair is on the shield, rather than only on the helmet crest. The blue shield on Cod.icon. 309, fol. 51r remains the one clear example in the project image records; Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573 remains a visual candidate whose animal identification is uncertain.

A useful additional comparison is the arms of Marschalk Wernher von Hüneburg in Strasbourg, BNU, ms. 739, fol. 5v, dated after 1391. The manuscript's scholarly catalogue describes one swan head in the shield's upper dexter field and two outward-turned swan heads in the crest. The digitized image is directly available in [Gallica](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10224576p); the [KdiH manuscript record](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/44/2/1) gives the folio, dating, and heraldic description. This is a strong parallel for the *outward-facing paired crest*, but it does **not** meet the question's shield-charge criterion.

## Armorial checks

- **Scheibler'sches Wappenbuch, BSB Cod.icon. 312c.** The project's cataloged image is the Rosenberg page; it shows the familiar striped shield and paired swan-neck crest. The searchable [Scheibler armorial index](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Scheibler_Armorial) describes a number of swan motifs, but the paired swan necks located in its entries are crest devices; I found no indexed blazon for an outward-facing pair as a shield charge. This is an index-based negative result, not a fresh folio-by-folio review of all 400+ arms.
- **Wappen besonders von deutschen Geschlechtern, BSB Cod.icon. 309.** I checked the additional project-recorded folios 7r and 15v against their page images. Their visible paired swan-like forms belong to crests, not shields. Fol. 51r is the known shield-charge example.
- **Donaueschinger Wappenbuch, Cod. Donaueschingen 496, fols. 81v and 83r; Ingeram Codex; Jörg Rugen's Wappenbuch, fols. 166v, 168r, 169v; and other armorial pages cataloged in `data/images/images.json`.** The project records provide isolated page images, not complete manuscript coverage. The records I could identify as swan-related point to Rosenberg-family arms or crest devices; I found no additional page that can be verified as an outward-facing pair on a shield. This does not establish that no such arms occur elsewhere in those manuscripts.

## Separate leads, not verified armorial matches

- A modern account of the lords of Mattstete/Mattstedt says their 12th–13th-century shield bore two outward-facing swan necks, with a further swan neck on the crest. I have not located a digitized armorial folio or a primary medieval image supporting this statement, so it remains a lead rather than an armorial find. See [Mattstedt](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattstedt).
- The arms of the Schwanewede family and municipality have two outward-facing, interlaced swan heads in the crest, while the shield carries one whole swan. They therefore do not match the requested placement. The coat's blazon and source trail are summarized in the [Wikimedia Commons record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:DEU_Schwanewede_COA.svg).
- Cod.icon. 309, fols. 7r and 15v, and the Hüneburg arms are relevant visual comparanda because they repeat outward-facing necks in crests. They should not be counted as shield-charge examples.

## Coverage and limits

I enumerated the armorial-labeled records in the project `data/images/images.json`, including the entries explicitly prioritized in the task (Scheibler, Ingeram, Donaueschingen, and Rugen), and checked the visual pages identified above. The JSON contains selected pages rather than complete manuscript runs. I also searched German blazon terms such as *zwei abgewendete Schwanenhälse*, *Schwanenköpfe*, and *Schwanenhälse im Schild*, then checked the closest manuscript lead against the specialist manuscript description. Thus the result is: **no additional verified page found in this pass**, not a claim that the motif is absent from every unscanned armorial.
