# Franconian monuments with outward-facing paired swans in the shield

## Finding

The criterion here is specifically **two swan heads/necks inside the shield, with their heads turned outward and away from one another**, as in the user's Cod.icon.309 image. This search found **no Franconian monument that can currently be confirmed to meet both the placement and orientation requirements**. The distinction matters: Rosenberg/Uissigheim arms commonly show a quartered or counterchanged red-and-silver shield, with two swan necks as the helmet crest. Crest pairs do not count, even when they are turned away from each other; the paired swans must be part of the shield/device itself.

The often-cited Rosenberg monuments at Wölchingen are therefore excluded. Their shields show the ordinary Rosenberg bars/panels; the swans belong to the crest. The same issue affects the Rosenberg funerary monument described in 1892: the account explicitly places “zwei Schwanenhälse” in the helmet crest and describes the quartered Rosenberg/Hirschhorn shields separately. It is useful evidence for the family crest, but not a two-swan shield.

## Franconian near-misses checked

* **Wölchingen (Boxberg), Arnold von Rosenberg (†1447) and Eberhard X von Rosenberg (†1449).** The monuments are documented and photographed, but their shields are the standard Rosenberg panels; the swan-neck device is in the crest. Thus, regardless of the crest pair's precise orientation, neither is an outward-facing paired-swan shield. Regional church and monument description: [Frankendom at Wölchingen](https://www.pro-region.de/de/proregion/dieregion/staedte_und_gemeinden/Boxberg_Kirchen_Frankendom.php). Photo collection: [Boxberg photographs, 15 May 2026](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Boxberg_(Baden)_photographs_taken_on_2026-05-15). **Excluded: crest only.**
* **Konrad von Rosenberg / Adelheid von Hirschhorn funerary monument, described in 1892.** The digitized *Zeitschrift für die Geschichte des Oberrheins*, vol. 46, describes the two swan necks as a helmet crest and identifies the shields as Rosenberg and Hirschhorn quarterings. Since the swans are not in the shield, their facing direction does not qualify them under this criterion. [Digitized volume (search within for “Konrad von Rosenberg” or “zwei Schwanenhälse”)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/64/Zeitschrift_f%C3%BCr_die_Geschichte_des_Oberrheins._Bd._046%2C_1892_%28IA_zeitschriftfurdi4618unse%29.pdf). **Excluded: crest only; the report does not establish the monument’s original location.**
* **Gnötzheim, Konrad von Rosenberg (†1596).** The local account documents an effigy and seven lineage arms above it but does not say that any shield contains swans. [Martinsheim municipal church history](https://www.martinsheim.de/kultur-freizeit/geschichte/chronik-gnoetzheim.html). **Unverified lead, not a find.**
* **Bamberger Dom, Schönau arms on the 1622 and 1629 memorials.** A recent article describes the Schönau shields as divided with three rings and the two swan necks as helmet crests. [Article with memorial descriptions and illustrations](https://ahnp.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/journals/breisach/article/view/114438/110167). **Excluded: crest only, even though this is a confirmed Franconian monument; outward orientation of a crest pair does not satisfy the shield requirement.**
* **Georg Kühlewein epitaph (1593–1656), St. Johannis, Magdeburg.** Search results identify the family as Franconian in origin and the epitaph as carrying its arms, but the two swan necks are described on the helmet, where they hold a grape; the shield itself has the family’s vessels/krüge. [Kühlewein family reference](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BChlewein_%28Adelsgeschlecht%29). **Excluded: outside Franconia and crest only.**

## Other apparent matches that do not qualify

* **BSB Cod.icon. 309**, shown by the researcher, is a manuscript armorial image with two swan necks visibly inside a blue shield. It is an important armorial parallel but is not a monument, and its bearer/page identification remains to be established. [BSB manuscript viewer](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00002106).
* **BSB Cod.icon. 307, image 00573 / manuscript page 617**, labelled “Stachel,” also shows a paired-neck-like charge in the shield. It is an armorial drawing, not a monument; the shapes may be proboscides, and the monochrome image does not resolve tinctures. [Page-specific BSB viewer](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00020245?page=573).
* Search results for the Lords of Mattstedt describe two opposed swan necks in the shield, but Mattstedt is in Thuringia, and no qualifying Franconian monument was located in this search. [Mattstedt reference](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattstedt).

## Conclusion and research boundary

The evidence currently supports a clear negative result for the narrow monument question: **no Franconian monument located in this search shows a shield/device with two swan heads turned outward, away from one another**. The Franconian monuments with paired swan necks located so far place them in the crest while their shields carry other charges. The user's Cod.icon.309 image shows the desired outward-facing arrangement in a shield, but it is an armorial image, not a monument, and it does not yet identify a bearer or connect the design to a Franconian funerary monument. No new monument find meeting the placement and orientation criteria emerged.

This is a catalog and indexed-source search, not proof that no such monument exists. A productive next step would be image-by-image review of Franconian epitaph inventories and church interiors, concentrating on any family or bearer eventually identified from the Cod.icon.309 caption. Until then, Wölchingen, Gnötzheim, and the Rosenberg–Hirschhorn stone should not be presented as two-swan shield monuments.
