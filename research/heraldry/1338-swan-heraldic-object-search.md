# Search for a 1338 swan heraldic object at Schweigern or Zwingenberg

## Result

I have not located a verifiable heraldic object dated 1338 that combines swan heads/necks with Schweigern (Boxberg), either Zwingenberg, or a similarly named family/place. In the sources found, “1338” belongs to a castle/political event at Zwingenberg am Neckar or to a documentary attestation of a Hünenberg witness. Neither source describes a swan-bearing object. The likely “near match” is a later 14th-century Hünenberg tomb slab at Kappel am Albis: it has a **single swan neck as a helmet crest**, while the shield itself bears two addorsed unicorn heads.

## Candidate records and objects

### Zwingenberg am Neckar: the 1338 event is not a heraldic object

The local-history entry for Zwingenberg reports the 1338 agreement/opening rights involving the castle and the archbishops/electors of Mainz and the Palatinate. This is a political history event, not a surviving 1338 seal, tomb, armorial, or sculpted heraldic object. LEO-BW’s separate civic-arms entry describes the later municipal arms as three silver swan necks in blue; the current municipal display must not be projected back onto the 1338 event without an object or contemporary blazon connecting them.

- LEO-BW, [Zwingenberg local-history entry](https://www.leo-bw.de/detail-gis/-/Detail/details/ORT/labw_ortslexikon/6203/Zwingenberg)
- LEO-BW, [municipal arms of Zwingenberg](https://www.leo-bw.de/en/detail/-/Detail/details/DOKUMENT/labw_wappen/6201/Wappen%2Bvon%2BZwingenberg): “In Blau drei (2:1) silberne Schwanenhälse mit goldenen Schnäbeln.” This is an arms record, not evidence that a 1338 monument bore the charge.
- Geo-Naturpark, [Zwingenberg am Neckar](https://geo-naturpark.net/kommune/zwingenberg-am-neckar/), repeats the 1338 castle-conflict context.

The related Fürstenstein was built by the Mainz archbishop in the conflict of 1338–40 and was subsequently demolished. The sources found describe surviving masonry remains, not extant heraldic sculpture or a swan-bearing artifact. The 1338 date therefore does not authenticate the municipal swan emblem as a contemporary object.

### Schweigern (Boxberg): no 1338 swan object found

Searches for Schweigern/Schwaigern with the date and heraldic variants did not turn up a 1338 seal, effigy, tomb slab, armorial, or other object with swans. Schweigern’s documented municipal arms are based on later Rosenberg-derived arms (bars and rose), not swans. This does not rule out an unindexed object, but no evidence currently supports the proposed 1338 connection.

- [Schweigern, LEO-BW](https://www.leo-bw.de/)
- Boxberg municipal information is the source cited for the Schweigern arms in the town’s local-history record; an easily discoverable summary gives the blazon as a divided shield with Rosenberg bars and a red bar with a gold rose: [Schweigern (summary)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schweigern).

### Hünenberg: a 1338 witness record is separate from the later swan-crest tomb

Gabi Meier’s archaeological and historical monograph on Hünenberg reports Heinrich von Hünenberg as a witness on 8 February 1338 to a settlement between Hof Arth and Austria (note 384, citing *Obwaldner Urkundenbuch* III.1, pp. 139–140, no. 203 and *Habsburgisches Urkundenbuch* II.1, p. 585). This is a person/date attestation only. It supplies no arms, seal, or swan image.

The same monograph separately illustrates the fragmentary grave slab of Gottfried IV von Hünenberg (d. 1383) and Margaretha von Friedingen (d. 1371), now in the former Cistercian church at Kappel am Albis. Figure 26 explicitly describes the Hünenberg shield as two addorsed unicorn heads, with a swan neck on the pot helm as a crest, and the Friedingen shield below. It is a documented later swan-crest monument, but it is not dated 1338 and does not have two swan heads in the shield. The figure caption gives Gottfried’s death as 1383, whereas the surrounding text says 1387; the date discrepancy should be checked against the original monument/inscription before using it as a precise dating anchor.

- Gabi Meier, [*Burgruine Hünenberg im Kanton Zug* (full digitized volume; see note 384 and fig. 26)](https://www.zg.ch/behoerden/direktion-des-innern/amt-fuer-denkmalpflege-und-archaeologie/publikationen/ftw-simplelayout-filelistingblock-1/burgruine_huenenberg_im_kanton_zug_sbkam_48_reduziert.pdf/%40%40download/file/Burgruine_Huenenberg_im_Kanton_Zug_SBKAM_48_reduziert.pdf)
- Bibliographic record and online edition: [Propylaeum e-books catalogue](https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeum/catalog/view/1259/2160/104627)
- Related primary-document edition (digitized *Urkundenbuch des Kantons Zug*): [PDF](https://www.histodata.ch/Quellen/UBZG.pdf). This is useful for checking the later Hünenberg documents, but does not turn the 1338 witness record into a swan-bearing object.

The PDF is the direct image-bearing source for figure 26, rather than a separate image-file URL. The figure is on the printed page corresponding to book page 68 (PDF page numbering may differ due to front matter). The caption is searchable in the PDF and states the location and heraldic details above.

## Other date/name traps checked

- **Schwaigern (near Heilbronn)** is distinct from Schweigern near Boxberg; no 1338 swan object surfaced in searches of this place-name either.
- **Zwingenberg an der Bergstraße** is distinct from Zwingenberg am Neckar and has a different civic emblem; no relevant 1338 swan object surfaced there.
- Search results for **Fürstenstein, 1338–40** concern construction and destruction in the Zwingenberg conflict, not a surviving armorial artifact.
- A 1338 illuminated grant of arms for Bonifatius/Egesius de Carbonensibus is unrelated to these places/families and does not bear swans; it was excluded as a false date match.

## Search coverage and limits

I searched exact and variant combinations including `Schweigern`, `Schwaigern`, `Zwingenberg`, `Zwingenberc`, `Zwingenburg`, `1338`, `Schwan`, `Schwanenhals`, `Schwanenkopf`, `Reiherhals`, and object terms such as `Siegel`, `Grabplatte`, `Grabmal`, `Wappen`, and `Armorial`. I checked web-indexed German heritage/local-history and monument references, LEO-BW, digitized historical literature/catalog records, and the Hünenberg archaeological monograph and cited documentary editions. Search engines and OCR are incomplete, and not every local/archive catalogue is fully indexed; this is a negative result within that coverage, not proof that no unindexed artifact exists.

## Assessment

At present, there is **no substantiated 1338 swan heraldic object** for Schweigern or Zwingenberg. The best new monument lead is the Kappel am Albis Hünenberg/Friedingen grave slab (later 14th century), which documents a single swan-neck crest. It should be treated as a separate comparison object, not as confirmation of the 1338 lead.

## Additional primary-seal lead: Wilhelm von Wimpfen / Zwingenberg

A materially relevant, earlier heraldic object does exist in the immediate Zwingenberg tradition: the seal of Wilhelm (Guillaume) von Wimpfen, dated 1240 in the Sigilla database. Sigilla describes the shield charge as “un cou et une tête de héron (Reiherhals), regardant vers la gauche” (a heron neck and head, facing left). This is a **single** bird neck/head, and the catalogue identifies it as a heron rather than a swan; it does not match the requested paired swan heads. It is nevertheless significant because a 1958 *Badische Heimat* article says Wilhelm’s frequently used seal showed “das Zwingenberger Wappen mit dem Reiherhals.”

- Sigilla record: https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/11189
- Direct seal image: https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/iiris_img/private/image/FRAD067_0151NUM0107_03.png
- *Badische Heimat* 1958, article PDF (passage on Wilhelm and his seal): https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/26164/file/BLB_Badische_Heimat_1958_2.pdf

The article also notes a 1253 Wilhelm von Zwingenberg describing himself as *nepos Wilhelmi advocati Wimpinensis*. A modern study cautions that this person cannot securely be identified with the Neckar-castle family because another Zwingenberg existed at Kochendorf near Wimpfen. The seal-to-Zwingenberg attribution in the 1958 article is therefore useful historical testimony, but the identification should not be expanded into a claim about the later 1338 event or a two-swan device. See Knauer, “Die Burgen der Grafen von Lauffen im Neckartal,” in *Heilbronnica 5*: https://stadtarchiv.heilbronn.de/fileadmin/daten/stadtarchiv/online-publikationen/heilbronnica5/online-publikationen-16-qf20-05-knauer-lauffen.pdf.

## Further query results and false positives

Queries run in this pass included `"1338" "Schwanenhals" Wappen Zwingenberg`, `"1338" "Reiherhals" Zwingenberg Wappen`, `"Schweigern" "1338" Ritter Grabmal`, `"Ritter von Zwingenberg" "1338"`, `"Herren von Zwingenberg" "1338" Schwan`, `"Zwingenberg" "1338" "Siegel" Burg`, `Schweigern 1338 Grabplatte Siegel`, `"zu Schweigern" 1338 Ritter`, and `"Schweigern" "Wappen" "Schwan" Mittelalter`.

The results reinforce two specific traps: (1) references to 1338 at Zwingenberg am Neckar describe political/opening-right restrictions on the castle and its tolls; the associated Fürstenstein counter-castle is dated around 1340, with documentary dispute records in 1340, not an extant 1338 tomb or armorial; (2) the place-name Schweigern occurs in unrelated 1338 records, including estates pledged by Heinrich de Fleckenstein, but the surfaced record has no swan-bearing image or relevant heraldry. The Schweigern court seal documented in 1516 instead displays Rosenberg-derived arms and another, unidentified half; this remains a separate, much later object, not evidence for the proposed 1338 bird arms. See the local seal summary: https://www.ortswappen.de/index.php?buchstabe=J&land=Baden-Wuerttemberg&site=10&site_i=15&wahl=6709.
