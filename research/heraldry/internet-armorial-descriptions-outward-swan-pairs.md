# German-language web search: outward-facing paired swan charges

## Question and criteria

Search for armorial descriptions and heraldic literature that describe **two swan heads/necks on the shield**, turned away from one another. Search terms included *zwei Schwanenhälse*, *zwei Schwanenköpfe*, *abgewendet*, *voneinander abgewendet*, *auswärts gerichtet*, *Schild*, and related variants. I excluded examples where the pair occurs only in the crest, and examples where the heads face one another. A blazon is evidence of the described arms; it is not by itself visual confirmation of an extant object or manuscript image.

## Positive and near-positive results

### Marschälle von Hüneburg / Huneburg (Alsace): strong historical blazon, but attribution and chronology need care

An 1880s digitized heraldic account of the Upper Alsatian nobility gives the arms as a horizontally divided black-and-gold shield, with “oben zwei abgewendete silberne Schwanenhälse mit rothen Schnäbeln”; it separately gives two silver swan necks as the crest. It also reports that older seals show only the divided shield and the two swan necks as crest, “also erst später in den Schild aufgenommen” (the necks were only later placed in the shield). This is a direct textual match for the requested shield motif, but the account itself describes an evolution, not a single invariant medieval form.

The same passage cites Herzog, Schöpflin, and other heraldic sources. A recent archaeological-history study of Hünenberg cautions that the family identity of the arms depicted by Schöpflin remains to be established and that separate Hüneburg/Hünenberg families should not be conflated. Thus record this as an Alsatian Hüneburg/Marschall arms tradition, not as evidence for the Swiss lords of Hünenberg or a Franconian family.

Sources: [digitized *Jahrbuch der K.-K. Heraldischen Gesellschaft Adler*](https://knihovna.archives.cz/arl-zao/en/csg/?key=2059401912&repo=zaorepo) (search-result transcription of the entry; the page is intermittently unavailable); [Meier, *Burgruine Hünenberg im Kanton Zug*](https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeum/catalog/view/1259/2160/104627), esp. discussion around note 228. Brief source wording: “oben zwei abgewendete silberne Schwanenhälse mit rothen Schnäbeln.”

### Herren von Mattstete / Mattstedt: reported medieval shield pair; image and primary evidence still need checking

The Mattstedt entry reports that the lords of Mattstete used “2 sich abgewendete Schwanenhälse im Schild” already in the 12th/13th century, with an additional single swan neck above the helmet. The page cites *Siebmacher’s grosses und allgemeines Wappenbuch*, city arms volume, 1885, p. 311, plate 299, and a local 1998 publication. This is a strong lead because the pair is explicitly assigned to the shield and the crest is distinguished from it. However, the medieval dating is currently encountered through a modern secondary page; the cited Siebmacher volume and its image should be inspected directly before treating the date or visual reading as verified. No tinctures are reported.

Source: [Mattstedt, with cited bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattstedt). Brief wording: “2 sich abgewendete Schwanenhälse im Schild.”

### Von Schwandegg (Zurich): exact blue-field parallel in a modern armorial compilation

A Swiss family-armorial database lists a Zürich lineage “von Schwandegg” with the blazon “In Blau zwei abgewendete, silberne Schwanenköpfe.” This is an exceptionally close verbal and tincture match to the user's Cod.icon. 309 image. It is described as a shield charge, not a crest. The page gives no date or historical source for this particular variant, and the image thumbnail should be checked at the linked page before assigning it historical weight.

Source: [Wappensammlung.ch, Schwandegg entry](https://www.wappensammlung.ch/wappensammlung/wappen-s-t/schupbach-seigneux?start=180). Brief wording: “In Blau zwei abgewendete, silberne Schwanenköpfe.”

### Niederhünigen (Bern): exact orientation, but municipal arms (modern)

The municipal blazon is “In Rot zwei abgewendete silberne Schwanenhälse mit goldenen Schnäbeln,” beneath a silver chief bearing a red star. The description explicitly says the motif derives from the older arms of Oberhünigen. This confirms the exact heraldic construction in a current Swiss civic arms, but does not establish a medieval family or armorial occurrence. The available blazon gives red, not blue, as the field.

Source: [Wikimedia Commons, Niederhünigen arms and blazon](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Niederh%C3%BCnigen-coat_of_arms.svg), citing the 1981 *Wappenbuch des Kantons Bern*. Brief wording: “in Rot zwei abgewendete silberne Schwanenhälse.”

## Excluded or weaker results

- **Rosenberg/Uissigheim:** their familiar outward-facing swan necks are described as crest figures above the divided/checkered shield. This is not an additional shield-charge parallel.
- **Von Behr (Gützkow/Pomeranian branch):** descriptions say the two outward-facing silver swan necks are on the helmet. Excluded as crest-only.
- **Schwanewede:** the outward-facing, crossed swan heads are explicitly a crest. Excluded.
- **Borggraefe:** a modern family registration gives two outward-facing gold swan necks in the shield, black field with checkered border. This meets the geometry and placement, but is a 1983 assumed/registered family arms, not a historical armorial discovery. [Eherold entry](https://eherold.org/emblem/wappen-borggraefe/).
- **Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573:** paired shapes seem outward-facing, but monochrome rendering and unresolved species identification keep it outside confirmed swan results.
- **Mülinen:** an 1868 account describes two inward-looking swan necks in a helmet crest, so excluded on both orientation and placement.
- Many broad web searches for “two outward-facing heads” return non-swan charges or pairs with inward-facing heads; the wording must be checked in context, especially whether it says *Schild* or *Helm/Helmzier*.

## Conclusion

The internet search finds credible additional **shield** parallels beyond Rosenberg/Uissigheim. The best historical candidate is the **Hüneburg/Marschall tradition**, whose later blazon explicitly places two outward-facing silver swan necks on a black-and-gold shield, although the source says this charge entered the shield later and the precise family attribution needs caution. **Mattstete** is a promising medieval-family lead explicitly describing the pair in the shield, but needs direct verification against Siebmacher 1885, p. 311, pl. 299 and its cited local source. **Schwandegg of Zurich** is the closest textual match in tincture and figure, though the consulted compilation does not establish a date. Niederhünigen is a firm modern civic parallel.

This is a targeted web search across German-language keyword variants and indexed digitized heraldic sources, not a complete search of all manuscript armorials or all catalogued Siebmacher entries. Historical armorial images for Mattstete and Hüneburg, and the source behind the Schwandegg variant, remain the next verification steps.
