# Paired swan charges in the project’s armorial images: a full-catalogue recheck

## Finding

This recheck found **no additional confirmed armorial** beyond the two examples already brought to attention: BSB Cod.icon. 309, fol. 51r, and BSB Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573. The first is clear: the shield itself is blue and bears two swan necks whose heads turn outward. Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573 is a monochrome, unresolved candidate. It shows a paired outward-facing shape in the shield, but this image alone does not let me identify the creatures securely as swans. It should not be counted as a confirmed swan achievement without a corroborating blazon, caption, or clearer parallel.

The broader scan did find many **lookalikes in crests**: paired swan necks or heads above shields, including Rosenberg examples. Those do not meet the requested criterion that the two swans be charges in the shield/device. The ordinary Rosenberg shield with its striped field and paired swans in the crest is especially common among these false positives.

## Verification method and limits

I reread all 153 records in `data/images/images.json`, selecting every record labeled `armorial` and every record whose title identifies a Wappenbuch, armorial, Wappentafel, tournament book, heraldic chronicle, or comparable heraldic image. This produced 63 candidate image records, including the two records whose region is named `goses` (Cod.icon. 309, 51r, and Cod.icon. 307, 573). I inspected each image itself, not only its title, region label, or image-index position. For each possible match I checked (1) whether the figures are recognizably swans, (2) whether there are two, (3) whether their heads point outward, and (4) whether they are inside the shield rather than above it as a crest. I also inspected the additional armorial-like records at entries 67, 129, 131, 146, and 147, which a search for the exact label `armorial` alone would miss.

For the two Munich manuscripts, I checked the BSB/MDZ digital catalogue records and their contents indexes. The catalogue identifies Cod.icon. 309 as a 164-page digitization and Cod.icon. 307 as a 740-page digitization; the full digitized records therefore contain many pages absent from this project’s image list. The conclusion here is about images represented in `images.json`, not an exhaustive leaf-by-leaf search of every page in every manuscript. The image list has multiple useful pages from Cod.icon. 307 and Cod.icon. 309, but the catalogue indexes do not identify a further two-swan shield among the pages represented here.

## Records checked

The 63 image records were entries 0–51, 53–58, 67, 129, 131, 146, and 147 in `data/images/images.json`. This intentionally includes manuscript and heraldic page images whose region name is `goses`, plus heraldic images whose label is absent or uses a different label. The remaining entries are not additional armorial page images based on their titles and descriptions.

The closest exclusions were:

- **Cod.icon. 307, fol. 573** (entry 49; the user’s monochrome image): shield-level paired outward-facing forms, but the species is unresolved from the scan. Candidate only.
- **Cod.icon. 307, fol. 642** (entry 50): the Uissigheim shield is striped; the swan-neck pair is the crest.
- **Cod.icon. 309, fols. 7r and 15v** (entries 26–27): heraldic page images, but no qualifying outward-facing pair of swans in a shield.
- **Cod.icon. 308 and 308u pages, Cod.icon. 311 and 312 pages, Donaueschingen 496, Jörg Rugen, Grünenberg, Richental, the Pfälzisches Wappenlehnbuch, and other checked armorial pages**: visible paired swan forms are crest devices or the shields have unrelated charges; none yielded another confirmed paired-swan shield.
- **Würzburg/Franconian armorial tables, Miltenberger Wappenbuch, Rüxner’s Thurnierbuch, Kilianshymnus, and the Schloss Grumbach heraldic frieze** (entries 53–54, 67, 131, 146–147): checked as additional heraldic-image records; no further shield meeting all four criteria.
