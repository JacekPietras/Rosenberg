# Armorial descriptions of outward-facing paired swan heads on shields

## Result

A positive match exists in a medieval German armorial record for **Jürgen (Jörg) von Rosenberg**, a Franconian family closely related to Uissigheim. The match had been missed in the earlier image-list audit. A searchable study of the Palatine fief armorial describes the shield recorded for Jürgen on fol. 128r as **a rose between two swan heads**, and identifies the heads as *addorsed* (back to back, therefore facing outward). The same study reports an earlier family blazon, recorded in 1323, as **Gules, two swan heads addorsed Argent**. This is a shield charge, not a crest.

The armorial’s own painted entry does not show the simpler 1323 arms: it is described as per fess and paly counterchanged, with a rose between the two swan heads and no crest ornament. The author explains that the Rosenbergs, a cadet of Uissigheim, had changed their arms to the per-fess and counterchanged-paly design. They originated at Rosenberg near Boxberg, bought Boxberg in 1381, and became extinct in 1632. This makes them a strong Franconian/Boxberg parallel to the requested motif, although the reported 1323 field is red, not blue.

## Main source: Palatine fief armorial

Steen Clemmensen’s open-access study identifies the manuscript as Karlsruhe, Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, GLA Karlsruhe 67/1057, *Lehensbuch des Kurfürsten Friedrich I. von Pfalz*. The manuscript is dated 1471 by LEO-BW; Clemmensen’s study presents a catalog of its arms. Its entry reads:

> “128r A G per fess & paly cch … no crest ornament OX-AG rose betw 2 swan’s heads”

Clemmensen then says the family “was a cadet of Uissigheim,” separated in the late thirteenth century, and was “noted 1323 with {Gu 2 swan’s heads addorsed Ar}.” The dated early blazon is therefore Gules, two outward-facing silver swan heads. The later armorial image’s rose between the heads is an additional shield charge. The notation explicitly says “no crest ornament,” preventing confusion with the familiar Rosenberg/Uissigheim crest pair.

- [Clemmensen, *An armorial of fief holders in the County Palatine*, PDF](https://www.armorial.dk/german/PfalzLehen.pdf), item 126, fol. 128r; see PDF pp. 47 and 124 for bibliography / concordance.
- [Searchable extract at the exact entry](https://www.armorial.dk/german/PfalzLehen.pdf#page=47).
- [LEO-BW manuscript description](https://www.leo-bw.de/fr/themenmodul/sudwestdeutsche-archivalienkunde/archivaliengattungen/amtsbucher/lehensbucher-und-lehensregister), dating the *Lehnbuch des Pfalzgrafen Friedrich I.* to 1471 and identifying GLA Karlsruhe 67 Nr. 1057.
- [Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek catalog record](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/RGBWEHPFTYHFPBRNL6OINMPB4HQN2PNK), for the same manuscript collection and its archive-provided digitization (different folio/item).

**Confidence:** High for the reading of the armorial entry and its separation of shield from crest; medium-high for the 1323 attribution, which is Clemmensen’s synthesis of the cited family literature rather than a facsimile of a 1323 seal in this volume. The image in the later armorial is 15th-century; 1323 is the earlier date supplied for the paired-head arms.

## Independent armorial cross-check: Rietstap’s Rosenberg entry

Rietstap’s *Armorial Général* includes a Rosenberg blazon with a rose between two *têtes et cols de cygne adossées* (swan heads and necks back to back). The searchable English translation places this pair in the **crest** for the principal variant, while the French scan’s punctuation around the alternate blazon is ambiguous as to whether its rose-and-head group belongs to the shield or the crest. For that reason, Rietstap is useful corroboration for the Rosenberg swan-neck tradition and the outward orientation, but it should not be cited alone as proof of a shield charge. Clemmensen’s fol. 128r record is the clearer shield-specific evidence.

- [Rietstap, Rosenberg entry, searchable transcription](https://www.coats-of-arms-heraldry.com/armoriaux/rietstap/blasons_ROSE_ROSE.html).
- [Digitized French Rietstap page, Rosenberg entry](https://www.heraldique-blasons-armoiries.com/armoriaux/rietstap/rosd_rose.pdf).

## Other shield examples found in armorial-description searches

### Marschälle von Hüneburg / Huneburg, Alsace

An Alsatian heraldic account describes a black-and-gold divided shield with two outward-facing silver swan necks, red beaks, in the upper half. It distinguishes these from the two silver swan necks in the crest and says older seals used the divided shield while the pair remained only in the crest; the heads were added to the shield later. This is a genuine shield-form parallel, but the paired charge is a later development, not the earliest seal form. Do not conflate this Alsatian family with the Swiss Hünenberg family.

- [Digitized *Jahrbuch der K.-K. Heraldischen Gesellschaft Adler* entry](https://knihovna.archives.cz/arl-zao/en/csg/?key=2059401912&repo=zaorepo), transcribed: “oben zwei abgewendete silberne Schwanenhälse mit rothen Schnäbeln.”
- [Meier, *Burgruine Hünenberg im Kanton Zug*](https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeum/catalog/view/1259/2160/104627), discussion around note 228, cautions that the arms depicted by Schöpflin need careful family attribution.

**Confidence:** High that this blazon is reported; medium for the attribution and historical sequence until the specific cited seals and armorial image are checked directly.

### Schwandegg, Zurich

A Swiss armorial compilation gives “In Blau zwei abgewendete, silberne Schwanenköpfe” (Azure, two outward-facing silver swan heads). This is the closest tincture-and-charge match to the blue-shield image in the conversation, and explicitly locates the heads in the shield. The page supplies no historical date or primary source for this family entry, so treat it as an armorial-database lead rather than a medieval attestation.

- [Wappensammlung.ch entry](https://www.wappensammlung.ch/wappensammlung/wappen-s-t/schupbach-seigneux?start=180), search within the page for “Schwandegg.”

**Confidence:** High for the wording on the consulted page; low-to-medium for antiquity or family attribution.

### Mattstete / Mattstedt

A modern article reports that the lords of Mattstete bore “2 sich abgewendete Schwanenhälse im Schild” in the 12th/13th century and cites Siebmacher’s 1885 city-arms volume, p. 311, plate 299, plus a local publication. No tinctures are given in the report. It is a useful additional historical-family lead, but the cited plate and local source need direct inspection before treating the medieval date as confirmed.

- [Mattstedt article and bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattstedt).

**Confidence:** Medium as a lead, low-to-medium as a verified medieval arms record until the cited sources are visually checked.

## Critical contrast: Hüneburg in the Uffenbachsche Wappenbuch

A scholarly manuscript-catalog record describes the full arms of Marschall Werner von Hüneburg in the Uffenbachsche Wappenbuch, fol. 5v (early 14th-century manuscript): the shield has **one** silver swan neck in its upper right; **two outward-facing swan bodies/necks occur in the crest**. This is not a two-swan shield charge. The distinction helps explain why some Hüneburg images appear to show a pair while the shield itself does not.

- [Bayerische Akademie der Wissenschaften, KdiH manuscript record](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/44/2/1).
- [Scholarly chapter on heraldic image and donor](https://elibrary.narr.digital/xibrary/10.24053-9783772054761).

## Conclusion for the blue-field image

The internet search now provides a firm Franconian shield-charge precedent: the Rosenberg family’s **Gules, two silver swan heads addorsed** arms, with a later armorial showing a rose between the heads. It also finds an exact **Azure, two silver outward-facing swan heads** wording for Schwandegg, but without a medieval date or primary-source trail in the consulted entry. These results establish that outward-facing swan heads on a shield are not confined to crests.

They do not by themselves identify the figure in Cod.icon. 309. Its blue field and two necks fit the Schwandegg description closely; its swan-head motif also has a close, historically grounded comparison in the Rosenberg branch descended from Uissigheim. Identifying the manuscript bearer still requires reading the original folio’s caption and neighboring entries and comparing its exact drawing and tinctures with those sources.
