# Monumental heraldry with outward-facing paired swan heads/necks

## Scope

This search counts a monument only when its **shield itself** bears a pair of swan heads/necks facing outward, away from one another, as in the user-provided BSB Cod.icon. 309 image. Inward-facing pairs, a swan used only as a crest or supporter, and armorial-only depictions do not qualify. The Rosenberg monuments at Wölchingen are excluded: their shields bear the familiar bars, while the swans are in the crest.

## Result: no qualifying monument confirmed

The broader search found no monument whose shield could be verified to carry outward-facing paired swan heads/necks. The strongest monumental near-match is the 1809 memorial of Anna Maria Vintler at St. Nikolaus, Hall in Tirol. Anton Eberle's survey describes a crowned, quartered shield whose fields 1 and 4 each show **two swan heads touching beaks** ("zwei Schwanenköpfe sich schnäbelnd"). Since they face one another, this monument does **not** match the required outward-facing orientation.

Eberle identifies the deceased as Anna Maria Vintler von Platsch und Rungglstein, a canoness who died 8 June 1809, and locates the memorial to the left of the church portal. The survey is an explicit contemporary description of the paired charge, but I found no photograph of this memorial. Its present-day survival is also unverified. It is retained here as an excluded comparator because it demonstrates the importance of checking orientation.

Source: Anton Eberle, “Grabsteine der St. Nikolaus-Pfarrkirche zu Hall,” *Zeitschrift des Ferdinandeums für Tirol und Vorarlberg*, vol. 20 (1876), pp. 1–42, especially p. 37 (article pagination; PDF pp. 36–37). The volume record and date are confirmed by [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Zeitschrift_des_Ferdinandeums_f%C3%BCr_Tirol.html?id=mQ375juZSLYC); [digitized article PDF](https://www.zobodat.at/pdf/VeroeffFerd_3_20_0001-0042.pdf).

## Other leads and excluded crest depictions

- **Warmbrunn, Schaffgotsch family chapel:** A historical transcription describes a painted wooden ancestral panel, since removed, with a red shield bearing two white swan necks and another pair in the crest. The text does not establish which depicted person/family owned this shield, whether the pair faced outward, or whether the panel survives. It cannot qualify. Source: [Warmbrunn, *Katholische Kirche – Grablege* (PDF transcription), pp. 20–21](https://jbc.jelenia-gora.pl/Content/37481/41212_Warmbrunn_Schaffgotsch_Sarge-4833.pdf).
- **Herren von Mattstede:** A modern summary says their 12th/13th-century shield had two outward-facing swan necks, but the search did not locate a specific monument image or a reliable monument record showing that shield. It is a family-arms lead, not a monument find. [Mattstedt article](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattstedt).
- **Herren von Huneburg (Alsace):** A heraldic account describes two outward-facing swan necks in the shield, but I found no associated surviving monument bearing that shield. Older seals reportedly show the swans in the crest instead; this is heraldic background, not a monument find. [Digitized *Jahrbuch der K.-K. Heraldischen Gesellschaft Adler*](https://knihovna.archives.cz/arl-zao/en/csg/?key=2059401912&repo=zaorepo).
- **Konrad von Rosenberg and Adelheid von Hirschhorn:** The nineteenth-century monument account mentions two swan necks only as the **helm crest** and separately describes the striped Rosenberg shield. This is excluded. See [*Zeitschrift für die Geschichte des Oberrheins*, vol. 46 (1892)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/64/Zeitschrift_f%C3%BCr_die_Geschichte_des_Oberrheins._Bd._046%2C_1892_%28IA_zeitschriftfurdi4618unse%29.pdf).
- **Ramschwag at Amtzell:** The epitaph's shield bears lions; three swan necks appear in the crest. This is a **crest depiction**, not a shield charge, and is excluded. [Ramschwag article](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramschwag_%28Adelsgeschlecht%29).
