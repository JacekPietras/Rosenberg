# Effigies and related portraits: Rosenberg, Uissigheim, Erligheim and Thüngen

Source: Ernst Cucuel and Hermann Eckert, *Die Inschriften des badischen Main- und Taubergrundes: Wertheim–Tauberbischofsheim*, Die Deutschen Inschriften 1, Heidelberg series 1, 1969 edition. [Local PDF](/Users/jpietras/Desktop/di001__z3.pdf). Digitization identifier supplied in the PDF: DOI `10.11588/diglit.53141`.

All 258 PDF pages were reviewed through the embedded transcription, with the personal-name, heraldic and illustration indexes crosschecked. Relevant illustrations were also inspected. The text layer contains Fraktur OCR errors; names and dates below follow the readable catalogue text and checked images. **PDF page numbers count from the first page of the supplied file**, including its digitization cover; printed page numbers refer to the book itself. Locations and condition are those reported by the volume, rather than a verification of present whereabouts.

The list contains **10 funerary catalogue entries**, including two monuments connected only through heraldry, and **two additional entries with non-funerary portraits**. Incised human figures are included alongside relief effigies. A joint monument is counted as one catalogue entry even when it portrays two people.

### Supporting passages and identification notes

- **109:** Gamans's preserved reading includes “Beatus Arnoldus iuvenis · miles · de Ussincke”; the editors translate the date as 14 November 1336. The commentary distinguishes the later murder legend from its interpretation of an execution. It also discusses a chronicle giving 1343 without a precise personal name; that discrepancy must not silently replace the inscription date. The slab is described as the cover of a raised tomb, with a question mark: *Deckplatte eines Hochgrabes(?)*.
- **117:** “Seine Gemahlin war Else von Erligheim.” This establishes the spouse connection, not a separate effigy of Else. The photograph confirms the male figure.
- **126:** “Stark abgetretene Umrißzeichnung einer Frauengestalt”; beside the head, “das Rosenberger und Tottenheimer Wappen.”
- **144:** “In der Art wie Nr. 145; auch die Wappen sind die gleichen”.
- **145:** “Die beiden Eckwappen bedeuten: Rosenberg und Erligheim.” The commentary's reference to his brother Arnold as no. 145 is a printed cross-reference error: Arnold's entry is no. 144.
- **212:** “Die beiden oberen Wappen sind als Tann und Thüngen aufzulösen, die unteren bleiben noch zu deuten.” The human figure is confirmed by the photograph, although the descriptive prose concentrates on heraldry.
- **220:** “Betende Frau in flachem Relief”; “Katharina, die erste Gemahlin Albrechts von Rosenberg”.
- **227:** “Reliefbild eines knienden Ritters vor einem Kruzifix”; “Zu seinen Füßen kniet ein zweiter, sehr kleiner Ritter”. The explicit armorial label is “ROSENBERG”.
- **239:** “In Hochrelief ein Ritterehepaar, einander zugewandt vor dem Kruzifix kniend”.
- **243:** “Im Gesamtaufbau und im einzelnen wie das Grabmal daneben (Nr. 215)”. The catalogue supports the joint monument and its comparison, while the identification of the intended comparison as no. 239 remains an inference.

## Findings by family

- **Rosenberg:** funerary entries **117, 126, 144, 145, 220, 239, 243**; heraldically related figural monument **227**; additional lifetime reliefs **21** and bust portraits **521**.
- **Uissigheim:** human effigy **109**; additional heraldic grave slab **108**.
- **Erligheim:** arms on **144 and 145**; **Else von Erligheim** named as the wife of the man depicted on **117**. **No separate effigy of a person identified as von Erligheim was found.**
- **Thüngen:** arms on the female effigy **212**, whose subject is named **Gute von der Thann**. **No funerary effigy of someone explicitly identified as a member of the von Thüngen family was found.** Jewel box **522** is an additional identified family object; priest **Ernst Thungen** in **308** is an uncertain family match and has no effigy.

Place-name matches alone were excluded: monuments located in Uissigheim do not automatically concern the noble family. Likewise, Erlenbach/Erlebach was not treated as Erligheim, and references to the Wertheim–Rosenberg archive were not treated as Rosenberg-family portraits.
