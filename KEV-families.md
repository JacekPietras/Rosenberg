# Rosenberg family classification reference

Research date: 2026-11-11. Three Luna subagents researched the eight requested
categories; the combined notes below distinguish source evidence from proposed
classification cues. This is a research reference, not a validated classifier
prompt or a family tree. The shared ChatGPT conversation could not be retrieved.
The descriptions received a further source review and targeted research round;
see the review status at the end.

## How to use the evidence

Match a person's styled name together with date, office, relationships, or an
activity involving a named place. A bare surname, given name, castle name, issuing
archive, or document location is weak evidence. People could serve or transact
far from their family's home territory. The categories are research groupings;
they do not establish the identity or genealogy of individual people.

The classifier needs particular care with `schenk` and
`brandenburg`: the research below does not establish every equivalence currently
assumed in their descriptions. Likewise, Mönch names in Prussian records need
separate investigation. Do not turn unresolved links into identifying rules.

## `franconian`: Bauland / Odenwald Herren von Rosenberg

**Repository source base.** The main detailed source for this profile is
[Bauer, Die Herrn von Rosenberg (1872)](data/books/bauer_1872.json), read in its
German text. Entries 3 and 5 describe the broad territorial footprint and
distinguish the ordinary Rosenberg lords from the Münch family. The account is
a reconstruction with explicit doubts about several pedigrees; classification
of a family does not require accepting every proposed parent-child connection.

**Core geography and distinction.** The relevant Rosenberg is in today's
Neckar-Odenwald district, with a later center at Boxberg. EBIDAT distinguishes
the 1251 witness *Monachus de Rosenberg* from the Uissigheim ministerial family
which acquired the castle before 1300 and adopted the Rosenberg name. This is
evidence against treating all early bearers as one established lineage.
[EBIDAT, Rosenberg, history by Thomas Steinmetz](https://www.ebidat.de/cgi-bin/ebidat.pl?id=5773)

**Origin is not a classification prerequisite.** The supplied family overview
qualifies the Uissigheim origin as a proposed derivation and dates attestations
at Rosenberg to 1285. Accordingly, retain the distinction between the named
families without presenting every step of their early descent as settled.
Matching a document does not require proving the Uissigheim pedigree.
[Franconian overview, origins discussion](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenberg_(fr%C3%A4nkisches_Adelsgeschlecht))

**Holdings and people.** The same source identifies the 1381 acquisition of
Boxberg and the subsequent shift of the family's center there. Wölchingen's
official place history explicitly records its passage with Boxberg to the
Rosenbergs in 1381. Albrecht von Rosenberg (died 1572), the Schüpfergrund and
Unterschüpf provide later context; the family overview also covers
Haltenbergstetten and Bartenstein. These are contextual anchors, not exclusive
place names.
[LEO-BW, Wölchingen](https://www.leo-bw.de/en/web/guest/detail-gis/-/Detail/details/ORT/labw_ortslexikon/3696/W%C3%B6lchingen),
[Franconian family overview](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenberg_(fr%C3%A4nkisches_Adelsgeschlecht)),
[LEO-BW, Albrecht von Rosenberg](https://www.leo-bw.de/detail/-/Detail/details/PERSON/wlbblb_personen/132268914/Rosenberg%2BAlbrecht%3B%2Bvon)

**A corpus anchor.** The source text already stored in
[1387-06-22.json](data/documents/1387-06-22.json) concerns Eberhard von
Rosenberg's ecclesiastical burial, the Wölchingen tithe dispute, Bishop Gerhard
of Würzburg, and death near Lauda. Its Latin text also names Konrad, Arnold,
and Engelhard. The combined setting supports a Franconian proposal; the given
names alone do not. This is a proposed reference case based on the stored source
text, not independent verification of its transcription or extracted facts.
[Catalog citation: GLA Karlsruhe 43 Nr. 5662](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/plink/?f=4-1804880)

**Office and court context.** Fouquet's study of King Ruprecht's finances names
Arnold von Rosenberg the Elder as Amtmann of Lauda in the 1401 credit campaign
and discusses Rosenberg officeholding at Heidelberg and Amberg. It separately
names Contz Mönch von Rosenberg among the king's agents. Palatine court service
therefore strengthens a combined identification but cannot by itself distinguish
the main family from Mönch bearers.
[Gerhard Fouquet, Die geliehene Zeit eines Königs (2022), discussion of February 1401 and notes 37–38](https://www.historischekommission-muenchen.de/fileadmin/user_upload/dateien/abteilungen/Schriftenreihe/fouquet-2022-die-geliehene-zeit-eines-koenigs.pdf)

An earlier regest dated 1350-02-25 concerns Eberhard von Rosenberg, Vogt at
Wildenberg/Wildenburg, his service to Mainz, and revenues around Mudau and the
Odenwald. This provides another direct name/office/geography combination beyond
the later Boxberg-centered history.
[Mainzer Erzbischofsregesten, Otto no. 5820](https://www.ingrossaturbuecher.de/id/source/13998)

**What the original short profile missed.** Bauer's German entries 17–21
describe Eberhard as Mainz's Vogt at Dürn/Walldürn, connect him with Nagelsberg
and Archbishop Balduin's affairs at Amöneberg, Geismar and Fritzlar, and
explicitly call him a Mainz Stiftsvormund in 1347. Entry 94 discusses Konrad
officeholders at Amberg and Heidelberg, while qualifying their placement in
the pedigree. Entries 18, 38 and 40 describe recurring fiefs and church rights
at Boffsheim/Boßheim, Sindolsheim, Burgheim, Schweinberg and other places.
These are useful family contexts alongside the familiar castles; service or
transactions outside the core territory do not by themselves imply another house.
[Bauer, entries 17–21, 38, 40 and 94](data/books/bauer_1872.json)

**Clerics need coverage too.** Bauer's entry 96 mentions Hans/Johann as a
Würzburg canon and Eberhard as a Mainz cathedral cantor, without resolving their
positions in his trees; he separately questions whether Hermann Rosenberg was
bourgeois. Bauer does not name Philipp of Speyer. The latter's office as
bishop is recorded in Stocker's entry 121, while Biedermann's entry 24 also
calls him provost of St German. Biedermann's dates differ, so that passage is
used here for the office/name combination, not its chronology or full pedigree.
[Bauer](data/books/bauer_1872.json),
[Stocker (1896), entry 121](data/books/freiburger_dioecesan_archiv_1896_25.json),
[Biedermann (1751), entry 24](data/books/bidermann_1751.json)

**Book-specific cautions.** Bauer's entry 8 quotes the 1288 style Eberhardus
de Rosenberg dictus de Ussinkheim; the wider inheritance/branch explanation in
entries 9–11 is his hypothesis. Entry 53 distinguishes Helmstadt co-owners
called Rosenberg. The struck-out identification in entry 7 is corrected as
confusion with Eckard von Rossy. Neither that identification nor uncertain
pedigree placements should become automatic family matches. In entry 49,
the construction concerning Ludwig Münch and Johann holding for children
must not be read as proving that they were Eberhard's sons.
[Bauer, German source entries 7–11, 49 and 53](data/books/bauer_1872.json)

**A misleading existing label.** The repository's
[1325-08-13 document](data/documents/1325-08-13.json) is labelled Franken,
but names Vogt Friedrich called the Frank and Ulrich/Konrad. Regional historical
research places that exact Burgau-service/wood-rights event with the Voit/Voyt
of Rosenberg near Sulzbach. The epithet Frank/Franch does not establish the
Bauland family. This is a label-review finding; the stored document was not
changed. A precise alternative choice in the eight-family taxonomy is not
treated here as established.
[VHVO 43, printed p. 140, Voithenberg family discussion](https://www.heimatforschung-regensburg.de/2792/1/1306233_1306528-1306821_pp_290.pdf)

## `schenk`: Schenk von Roßberg and Rosenberg renderings

**Secure nearby family.** The Schenk von Roßberg family, associated with today's
Schenkenschloss outside Würzburg, is well documented. The House of Bavarian
History dates its first records to the 1270s and describes Würzburg ministerial
service and the Mundschenk office. The castle was called Roßberg until around
1600. Search forms should include Roßberg, Rossberg and Rossenberg, while
retaining the exact source spelling.
[Haus der Bayerischen Geschichte, Schenkenschloss](https://hdbg.eu/burgen/detail/burgruine-schenkenschloss/144)

**Specific identifying combinations.** A 1337 deed description names Wolfram
Schenk von Rossberg, his brother Heinrich and Heinrich's son Berthold in a
purchase of Gadheim from the bishop. Conversely, a regional historical study
uses Eberhard Schenk von Rosenberg for a 1390 dispute concerning Burleswagen,
Roßburg and Haltenstetten tithes before the Würzburg court.
[DDB, 1337 deed](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/2T63FTP5LX5GMEDFLOOMQHSJHQSTRDSL),
[Crailsheimer Historischer Verein study, printed p. 94 and note 1058](https://crailsheimer-historischer-verein.de/workspace/media/documents/wollm11_zz3_www-5bca5f1ab910d.pdf)

**A useful edition comparison, with limits.** The source text stored in
[1363-04-14.json](data/documents/1363-04-14.json) contains two accounts of the
same 8,000-gulden Langensalza transaction: the older regest names Eberhart
Schenk von Rosenberg, while the appended text identified as RIplus Regg. EB
Mainz 2.1 no. 1624 names Eberhard Schenk von Roßberg, with Rosperg/Rossberg
variants. It separately names Hans and Eberhard von Rosenberg. This supports
investigating editorial name variation rather than assuming one family from
every Rosenberg spelling. The independent modern item page was not recovered
in this round, so the comparison's immediate evidence is the stored text.
[Older edition cited by the document](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10333801?page=81)

**Other checks and unresolved boundary.** A 1363 Bamberg catalog record names
Eberhart Schenk von Rossberg/Rosperg and his wife Agnes. A separate 1492–1493
catalog record calls Jörg Schenk von Rosenberg the husband of Margaretha von
Thüngfeld. The 1537 Fries entry names Georg Schenk von Roßberg, son Christoph,
and Margaretha von Thüngfeld: its transcription and person metadata identify
her as Georg's wife and Christoph's mother, despite an ambiguous modern summary.
The Jörg/Georg-and-Margaretha resemblance is a lead, not a proven identity.
[GDA, Kloster Michelsberg Urkunden 421](https://www.gda.bayern.de/gvl/document/71d2d482-50ab-4d1f-9d04-501aca6f51ab),
[GDA, Neuverzeichnete Akten Bamberg 7143](https://www.gda.bayern.de/gvl/document/1f67e634-f54d-46c1-89d8-f6b3fd00344a),
[Fries, Rosberg 1537, entry 5716](https://www.historisches-unterfranken.uni-wuerzburg.de/fries/fries-results.php?eintrag=5716)

The material establishes a Schenk von Roßberg house and some evidence of
Rosenberg renderings; it does not establish a second coherent, genealogically
separate Schenk von Rosenberg house. Original-image comparison is still needed
for the 1492 file and the 1390 record cited above. Keep `schenk` as a working
category with contextual identification, rather than universal spelling
normalization. Ordinary Schenk offices, other Schenk houses, and the German
verb *schenken* do not establish membership. This is the classification
recommendation drawn from the evidence above.

## `monch`: Mönch / Münch von Rosenberg

**Early and southwestern context.** The official Rosenberg place history
distinguishes two lower-nobility families named after the same place: Mönch von
Rosenberg, recorded from 1251 into the Thirty Years' War, and von Rosenberg,
recorded 1276–1632. EBIDAT gives the early Latin style *Monachus de Rosenberg*.
These sources support a separate category without requiring a reconstructed
pedigree between every later bearer.
[LEO-BW, Rosenberg in the Neckar-Odenwald district](https://www.leo-bw.de/en-GB/detail-gis/-/Detail/details/ORT/labw_ortslexikon/6163/Rosenberg%20-%20Altgemeinde~Teilort),
[EBIDAT](https://www.ebidat.de/cgi-bin/ebidat.pl?id=5773)

**Later anchors.** Mönchzell's history describes Mönch/Münch von Rosenberg as
Katzenelnbogen vassals from 1354. Lutz and Konrad, Hans, Dieter, Ruprecht and
Peter occur in successive periods. Its descriptions connect some bearers with
Eberbach, Dallau, Steinsberg and Kirchardt. These combinations help distinguish
southwestern records; the list is not a license to merge repeated names.
[Mönchzell history and named holders](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6nchzell)

**Prussian namesake warning.** Bernhart Jähnig describes Gerlach Mönch von
Rosenberg, an Osterode commander, as belonging to an Elbing family probably
immigrant from Lübeck, related to the Pomesanian bishop Johann Mönch. That
account does not establish descent from the southwestern Mönchs. Consequently,
the classifier's existing blanket rule that explicit Mönch/Münch always
identifies one family is too broad for all geographical contexts.
[Jähnig, Die Osteroder Komture des Deutschen Ordens und ihre Laufbahnen, ZfO 36 (1987), p. 385 and note 24](https://www.zfo-online.de/portal/zf/article/download/4895/4894)

## `rorschach`: eastern Swiss Rorschach / Rosenberg

**Identity, place and period.** The Historical Dictionary of Switzerland
explicitly treats von Rorschach and von Rosenberg together: a knightly family
of the 12th–15th centuries serving St Gallen's prince-abbots. Centers include
Rorschach, Herisau, Rosenberg, Rosenburg, Urstein, Berneck and Zuckenriet. Egilolf
and Rudolf are attested as witnesses in 1176. Repeated use of those names makes
individual genealogy difficult. The connection of the Rosenberg-named people
recorded in 1222 to particular branches remains uncertain. Egilolf the Elder
received Berneck in 1305; HLS gives branch endpoints of 1427 for
Rosenberg-Berneck and 1445 for Rosenberg-Zuckenriet.
[HLS, Martin Leonhard, von Rorschach](https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/020308/2010-11-11/)

**Classification inference.** Rorschach/Rosenberg plus St Gallen service or
the named seats is useful evidence. Egilolf, Eglolf, Egloff and Egli are candidate
search forms; confirm the particular bearer rather than making all such names
synonyms. The supplied article's “Swabian” description and the project's “Swiss”
label need not imply different families: the article concerns the same Lake
Constance/St Gallen setting.
[Supplied Rorschach article](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorschach_(schw%C3%A4bisches_Adelsgeschlecht))

**Direct medieval anchor.** A deed of 1350-01-07 names the brothers Rudolf and
Rudolf von Rorschach, respectively parish priests of Rorschach and Herisau,
and their *Vetter* Eglolf von Rosenberg. It concerns Rosenburg and the Herisau
Meieramt. This supports the shared family grouping without requiring a precise
modern translation of the kinship term. The edition identifies the original as
Stiftsarchiv St Gallen, T2 A17.
[Chartularium Sangallense VII, no. 4158](https://stadtarchiv.ch/inhalt/CS_Bd_07.pdf)

**Attested short form.** A published regest dated 1407-01-12 calls a man
Egli von Rosenberg zu Bernang im Rheinthal. This supplies a name/place combination
for searches; it does not establish that every Egli elsewhere belongs to this
family. Retain the historical Bernang form alongside modern Berneck.
[Thurgauische Beiträge 21, printed p. 87, no. 227](https://hvtg.ch/skin/frontend/PDF/publikationen/TextTB021.pdf)

**Chronology needs care.** Vannotti's study reports Egli von Rosenberg
transferring part of Berneck to his son-in-law Konrad Payer and Konrad's brother
in 1431, citing Stiftsarchiv St Gallen 1747, fols. 123v–124v. This sits uneasily
with the HLS endpoint of 1427. The original transfer needs checking before
resolving the discrepancy. Do not turn an overview's branch endpoint into a
hard exclusion of later Swiss Rosenberg records or references to inherited
property.
[Barbara Vannotti, Von der entlaufenen Nonne zur Schlossherrin: Magdalena Payer von Hagenwil, discussion and note 18](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/19874/file/BLB_Vannotti_Magdalena_Payer_von_Hagenwil.pdf)

**Service abroad.** Hamburg's Prussian regesta record the Teutonic Order brother
Eglolff von Rosemberg writing from St Gallen on 1455-02-26. The following day's
intercessors include Eglolff von Roschach. This is concrete evidence of a Swiss
network around a Rosenberg Order brother, although it does not prove his exact
descent from a particular Rorschach branch. Prussian service should not exclude
Swiss identification; neither should it establish it without further context.
[PrUB, JH I 13540–13541](https://www.spaetmittelalter.uni-hamburg.de/Urkundenbuch/pub/orden1455.html)

## `czech`: South Bohemian Rožmberks

**Names, origins and seats.** Forms include von Rosenberg, *de Rosis*, *z Růže*
and *z Rožmberka*. This is the South Bohemian Witigon house. Wok I, who died
in 1262, founded the Rosenberg/Rožmberk castle after 1225 and Hohenfurt/Vyšší
Brod monastery in 1259. The family inherited the Krumlov line's holdings at the
beginning of the 14th century and moved its main seat to Český Krumlov.
[NDB, Annemarie Enneper, Rosenberg](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118973339.html)

**Fourteenth-century people.** After Petr I's death in 1347, his surviving sons
included Petr II/Peter (died 1384), Jošt I/Jost (1369), Oldřich I/Ulrich (1390)
and Jan I/Johann (1389). Their elder brother Jindřich II had died in 1346.
These combinations are useful dated context. Šimůnek also documents Jošt's
chamberlain office and Jan and Oldřich accompanying Charles IV to Nuremberg in
1355: a German setting does not make them Franconian Rosenbergs.
[Robert Šimůnek, Karel IV. a Rožmberkové, printed pp. 70–78](https://rukovet.cms.flu.cas.cz/static/Odkazy_upload/HT_17_2012.pdf)

**Passau context.** Petr I acquired Haslach in the Austrian Mühlviertel as a
Passau fief in 1341. Šimůnek's study cites the charter edition RBM IV, p. 400,
no. 1005. This is a concrete reason to retain Czech identification in some
Passau/Upper Austrian transactions.
[Šimůnek, Správní systém šlechtického dominia](https://obchod.hiu.cas.cz/user_uploads/Knihy%20ke%20sta%C5%BEen%C3%AD/spravni_system_slechtickeho_dominia_simunek.pdf)

**Later people and traps.** Ulrich II (1403–1462), Wilhelm/Vilém (1535–1592), and
Peter Wok/Petr Vok (1539–1611) are useful dated anchors. Peter Wok was the last
male-line representative. Ulrich II constructed a fictive Roman Orsini descent;
Orsini language therefore does not automatically imply the Austrian category.
The family's dealings extended beyond Bohemia: an Austrian setting should not
itself reject this identity. Wok/Vok is a useful search form, but distinguish
the 13th-century founder from later bearers of that given name.
[NDB family article](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118973339.html),
[Supplied Bohemian overview](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenberg_(b%C3%B6hmisches_Adelsgeschlecht))

## `lengenfelder`: Rosenberg near Sulzbach

**Working scope.** The user supplied Lengenfelder, Lengenfelderin, Lengenfeld
and Lengenvelder as related forms for this category. A local-history biography
uses Heinrich der **Lengfelder** von Rosenberg and places him as Burgmann at
Rosenberg near Sulzbach in 1314; add Lengfelder to searches. This identifies a
historical bearer and setting, not a securely established genealogy for every
name variant.
[Heinrich der Lengfelder von Rosenberg, with local-history references](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_der_Lengfelder_von_Rosenberg)

**Stronger fourteenth-century anchors.** The 1343-02-21 Kastl sale names
Chunrad/Chunrat der Lengenvelder von Rosenberg and wife Jeute, involving
Pfeffershoven/Pfeffertshofen. A printed historical account reproduces the
Rosenberg qualifier and relates the transaction to Sulzbach and Kastl.
This is a stronger contextual example than an unqualified surname.
[GDA, Kastl document](https://www.gda.bayern.de/gvl/document/223f7d80-959c-45d5-9bad-0c896b55b238),
[VHVO 89, printed p. 106](https://www.heimatforschung-regensburg.de/2739/1/865353_865761-866168_pp_407.pdf)

The catalog abstracts preserved in this repository add a useful series:

The live GDA pages could not be inspected in this round; the corresponding

and [1359](data/documents/1359-04-01.json). These abstracts support the
classification context. They do not independently prove that every Konrad or
Jeute/Jeutt in the series is the same person, or connect this group to Heinrich
of 1314. Treat Lengenfelderin as an attested feminine form within this context.

**Conflicting identifications.** Norbert Beutler's regional study distinguishes
the Rosenberg Burgmann from Heinrich of Lengenfeld near Tirschenreuth. It
challenges the attribution of the 1325 Hirschau/Ehenfeld grant to the Rosenberg
man and instead associates it with the Tirschenreuth family. The study also
separates other Lengenfeld namesakes. This is a documented disagreement with
popular summaries; the original records should decide individual cases.
Surname variants alone cannot resolve it.
[Beutler, Die Lengenfelder, especially printed pp. 17 and 31–33](https://www.heimatforschung-regensburg.de/3450/1/Die%20Lengenfelder%20I.%20%20HVOR.pdf)

Beutler also treats a 1321 Konrad-and-Jeuta marriage as ambiguously attributable
among Lengenfeld groups. Its similarity to the 1343 couple's names is not proof
of identity. His 1325 identification is an argument from property succession
and kinship, so preserve that attribution as his interpretation rather than
silently adding a locality to the royal record.

## `orsini`: Austrian Orsini / Ursini-Rosenberg

**Medieval coverage.** A heraldic study hosted by the Styrian state archive
discusses a sequence of styles *de Valle*, von dem Graben, and *ab dem Rosenberg*
in 1322, including a seal dated 1322-09-06. This supplies a Styrian/Graz lead for
medieval Rosenberg records, although the exact early parentage still requires
documentary confirmation. The later Orsini double name is not expected in such
a medieval source; absence of that name or an early date cannot by itself
exclude an Austrian Rosenberg attribution.
[Erwin Naschenweng, Der landständische Adel des Herzogtums Steiermark, version 2020, Orsini und Rosenberg entry](https://www.landesarchiv.steiermark.at/cms/dokumente/12799919_160291652/a7a9fa8a/NASCHENWENG%20Webseite%20Version%20Oktober%202020.pdf)

**Dated identifying context.** The NDB describes a Carinthian family whose
Johann Andreas (1600–1667) entered the Carinthian estates in 1621, became a baron
in 1633 and count in 1648. His sons included Georg Nikolaus (1623–1695) and
Wolfgang Andreas (1626–1695). Estate names include Lerchenau, Mageregg,
Grafenstein, Maria Loretto, Rosegg, Rottenstein, Stein im Drautal and Greifenberg.
Leopold I authorized the Ursini name in 1684; Franz Xaver Wolf (1723–1796)
became a prince in 1790. Records before 1684 may use bare Rosenberg for this
family.
[NDB, Herbert Knittler, Orsini und Rosenberg](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz73780.html)

**Ancestry caution.** The Roman descent and the claimed connection to the
extinct Bohemian Rosenbergs were prestige constructions. Compare the actual
person, period and possessions. A scholarly study treats both the Bohemian
and Austrian Orsini appropriations; it should prevent a model from interpreting
the shared claim as proof of one family.
[Petr Maťa, The false Orsini from over the Alps](https://www.oeaw.ac.at/fileadmin/Institute/INZ/img/menschen/Mata/The_false_Orsini.pdf)

## `kamptal`: Lower Austrian Lords of Rosenberg

The book overview in `data/books/ai_2026.json` distinguishes this local
ministerial family from the other Rosenberg houses. It places the family at
Rosenberg Castle (Rosenburg am Kamp) in the Kamptal near Horn, with attestations
from 1175 to 1290. The named bearers are Gozwin/Goczwin/Godezwin (1175), Konrad
(1229, in a Zwettl transaction), and Hartlieb (1290). Use the Kamptal/Rosenburg
am Kamp setting together with a Rosenberg bearer or explicit seat/family
connection; the common surname or castle name alone is not an attribution.

This is distinct from the Styrian/Carinthian Orsini-Rosenberg house and from
Rosenberg castles elsewhere. The dates and named members are the book's summary
of source attestations, not proof of a complete pedigree or of descent between
these three bearers.

## `brandenburg`: separate the Brandenburg records from Saale Rosenburg

**Directly inspected 1338 agreement.** CDB II/2, p. 140, document DCCLV,
records Markgraf Ludwig's protection of Rosenberg and Bredow bearers with
castle Plate on 1338-10-17. The printed Latin names Johann and two
Werner/Wernher forms of Rosenberg, alongside Peter, Copke, Willekin and
Matheus of Bredow. This establishes a direct association and a named holding.
The wording calls Johann *frater* and has *fratres* after the Werner forms;
it should not be flattened into a proven three-person sibling group without
resolving that syntax and Johann's possible religious title. The printed scan,
not the medieval original or copy-book, was inspected.
[CDB II/2, p. 140, DCCLV, Paderborn scan](https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/ihd/content/zoom/4232479)

**Correction to the later summary.** The Bredow association describes a 1338
acquisition with two Rosenberg brothers. The inspected agreement names three
Rosenberg bearers and records protection, not a sale. The purchase may have
other supporting records, but this deed alone does not prove it. Neither source
establishes the Rosenbergs' home castle or a Bredow marriage.
[Familienverband von Bredow, Altenplathow section](https://familie-von-bredow.org/gueter/)

**Name-index evidence has limits.** The CDB index lists Rosenberg spelling
variants and Johann/Werner bearers, including a 1338 group and Werner/Johann in
1400. It separately indexes the castle near Sulzbach. An index gathers names;
it does not establish that all indexed people belong to one lineage. Compare
the referenced deeds before using the entries as a pedigree or linking them
to Saale Rosenburg.

**Directly inspected 1400 property records.** CDB A XXIV, pp. 395–396, gives
instruments dated 1400-07-13 and 1400-09-29 naming Werner and Jan/Johann
Rosenberg as brothers. They concern the sale of Cutzekowe/Kutzkow, held from
Magdeburg, to the bishop of Brandenburg, with repurchase terms. Page 398
records a former holding of Jan von Rosenberg at Luge. These are useful northern
name/property/lordship combinations. The 62-year interval and reused names do
not establish continuity with the 1338 group or biological distinctness from
every other Rosenberg house.
[CDB A XXIV, pp. 395–398, scanned edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=I33GGQyrVX0C&pg=PA395)

**Saale Rosenburg is not yet linked.** The state archive identifies Rosenburg
as a Magdeburg fief of the counts of Barby; Göttingen's academic reference dates
the expansion of Barby across the Saale to Rosenburg to 1306. Neither source
establishes that the Rosenberg brothers associated with Bredow were that family.
This missing bridge must remain explicit.

**Burchard requires his own identification.** Heckmann's Teutonic Order office
list records Burkhard von Rosenburg as Vogt of Kulmerland in 1338–1340 and
Burchard von Rosenburg as Vogt of Leske in 1345. Its index distinguishes this
name from Rosenberg bearers. Older scholarship tentatively associates the Leske
official with the Magdeburg/Barby Rosenburg family. That is not proof that he
belongs to the Brandenburg/Bredow group.

## Consequences for the classifier

These are proposed changes to evaluate, not measured accuracy improvements:

- Expand the Franconian reference to include source-grounded Wölchingen/Lauda
  combinations and distinguish bare place mentions from family activity.
- Preserve the Schenk/Roßberg question and the Brandenburg/Rosenburg question
  as unresolved rather than asserting an unsupported genealogy.
- Permit Czech and Swiss family members to appear outside their home regions.
- Separate the historical Orsini ancestry claims from the Austrian family name.
- Treat explicit Mönch as a name cue whose lineage still depends on context.
- Use nine family choices plus one broad `other` category for all non-target
  material. The user selected this broader category over a place-only option.
- Retain the full distribution instead of `mixed` or `uncertain` answers.
  For multiple target families, choose the most directly represented; the scores
  are not independent family-presence probabilities or calibrated confidence.
- Do not interpret `von`, `de`, or `zu` alone as family or residence evidence.

The classifier does not automatically read this file. Its default prompt
contains a concise research-derived reference. A further investigation read all
169 German entries of Bauer's book and checked missing offices and clerics.
Adding long Franconian summaries to the model state caused systematic false
Franconian predictions, so those variants were rejected. The detailed source
research above is retained for review rather than injected wholesale.

The selected nine-choice prompt (before `kamptal` was added) matched 43/45 fresh references versus 29/45 for
the earlier eleven-choice prompt; Franken improved from 8/20 to 19/20.
See the [comparison and limitations](reports/kev-place-choice-2026-11-11/comparison.md).
This measures agreement with provisional references, not calibrated confidence
or a validated automatic labeling policy. The disputed 1325 Voit record remains
a model error even though its prediction agrees with the current Franken label.
