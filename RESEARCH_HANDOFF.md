### Essingen St Wendelinus chapel: source interpretation clarified

The local Heimatverein mural description explicitly identifies the faint swan-neck forms to the left of the north-wall sacrament niche as “die Schwanenhälse des Rosenbergwappens” of local lord and episcopal bailiff Balthasar von Rosenberg. It describes the painted hierarchy as king, clergyman, Balthasar, and a kneeling peasant paying tribute. On the west wall it identifies Balthasar as the third of the Magi, shown in close-fitting hose and ermine. Source: https://heimatverein-st-wendelinus-essingen.de/wandmalereien-im-altarraum/.

The Rosenberg connection is directly stated by the Heimatverein. Art historian Joachim Glatz, *Mittelalterliche Wandmalerei in der Pfalz und in Rheinhessen* (1981), dates the choir-tower murals “um 1470” (printed p.106; scan p.134): https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/glatz1981/0134. Glatz's Essingen description covers north/south biblical scenes and the west-wall Adoration of the Magi (printed p.197; scan p.225) but does not identify Balthasar or Rosenberg arms: https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/glatz1981/0225/text_ocr. The official *Tag des offenen Denkmals* listing calls the murals 15th-century; the Protestant parish describes them as late Gothic. These support a 15th-century cycle, with Glatz's c.1470 date the narrowest source found. No source located dates the north-wall heraldic detail separately from the cycle.

The Balthasar identification is therefore locally explicit but chronologically unresolved: the Heimatverein timeline places him as lord by 1530 and records his death as 22 April 1554, while another secondary source gives 22 February 1552. A primary 1546 record documents Balthasar settling a contribution toward the chapel's roof repair (Glasschröder, *Urkunden zur Pfälzischen Kirchengeschichte*, no.442, p.198): https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/glasschroeder1903/0198. It does not mention murals or painting and cannot date them. Glatz does not name Balthasar, the Rosenberg arms, or the tribute-paying peasant. No conservation stratigraphy report was found to determine whether the armorial/figural feature is a later layer or a local misidentification. Describe the arms as “identified by the Heimatverein as Balthasar von Rosenberg's”; do not present the figure as an independently verified contemporary portrait. The west-wall photo is https://heimatverein-st-wendelinus-essingen.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Wendelinus_Westwand_2-2048x1211.jpg and north-wall photo https://heimatverein-st-wendelinus-essingen.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Wendelinus_Nordwand_2-1889x2048.jpg; photographer/license are not stated. The chapel is in Essingen in the Palatinate; do not merge it with `essingen-st-quirinus` near Aalen.
